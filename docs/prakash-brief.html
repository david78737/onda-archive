ONDA Guide · Concept Brief · July 2026

Users Deserve Better Than This

A conversation with Prakash Chandran · Friday

The Problems

  • 1
    Photos in manuals go stale
    Visual references go out of date. Easy to print, impossible to maintain.
  • 2
    Video moves too fast
    People need a static image they can study to orient themselves. Video is hard to update and impossible to pause at the right moment.
  • 3
    Text has limits
    Not everyone reads well. Language barriers are real. Voice + text together transcend both.
  • 4
    ONDA voice transcription is painless
    Speak and the text exists. Accurate, immediate, no extra step. Voice and text arrive together.
  • 5
    Controls come and go — and move around
    Software updates constantly in the field. A control that was here is now there, or gone entirely. Any guide that can't be updated as fast as the software changes becomes a liability, not a resource.
  • 6
    People change jobs — knowledge walks out with them
    Institutional knowledge lives in individuals, not systems. When they leave, the organization starts over.
  • 7
    Corporate budgets make good documentation unlikely
    Documentation is always the last thing funded. The solution can't require a budget line. It has to be crowdsourced from the people who actually have the knowledge.
  • 8
    Video platforms optimize for revenue, not comprehension
    YouTube's economic model puts the learner at a disadvantage. Ads, algorithm, distraction — all working against understanding. When you want to go deeper, the platform sends you somewhere else.

The Insight

A tool accessible to anyone
Imagine a tool that allows anyone to document and monetize what they know. Not a free-for-all like YouTube — curated for accuracy and relevance. Words, pictures, voice. Financial incentives to supplement and maintain corporate value.

The person who understands a tool deeply — whether they're on the payroll or not — can publish what they know, earn from it, and keep it current. The business benefits without funding a documentation department. The knowledge stays alive because the incentive to maintain it stays alive.

What One Step Captures

Screenshot + Where to look + Voice + Transcript

The learner sees the exact image, hears the explanation, reads the words — at their own pace, in their own context. All three at once. Nothing built today does that.

Where It Lives — ONDA Nile

  • No budget required. Any expert can author a guide. Knowledge is crowdsourced from the people who have it.
  • Curators surface the best. Human judgment — not an algorithm — decides what's worth following.
  • Tags navigate across platforms. One tag surfaces guides across Xano, Excel, IRS forms — whatever tools a task requires.
  • No ads. No algorithm. The learner follows the thread as deep as they need to go.
  • This is not Stack Overflow. Curators build a guild — a network of contributors whose collective knowledge is worth more than any individual piece. No anonymous voting. No stale answers. Human accountability at every level.
The questions
Can a lightweight plugin record where an expert's attention goes — across any interface — and turn that path into something anyone else can follow?

And when a learner follows that path — where does the guide live? A second screen? A browser side by side? An overlay on the interface itself? How does the learner make their screen match what the expert is showing them?

One possibility: a drawer that lives on the edge of any browser screen — context-aware, always accessible, never pulling you away from what you're doing. But that's one idea. How do you see it?
