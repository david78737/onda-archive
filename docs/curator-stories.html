Three Ways a Curator Station Works

A Curator station on ONDA Nile is a curated library of audio content — searchable, playable, and shareable — built from the voices of people you know and trust. This document describes three curator scenarios. Links to the actual forms are included so you can see what the workflow looks like in practice.

How identity works across ONDA Nile

A contributor's phone number is their identity across the entire system. It carries across events, stations, and curators. One contributor may be enrolled in PCA35, have presented at PCA28, and contribute to a completely separate station — all under the same phone number.

When they open ONDA Replay and enter their phone, the OTP verification surfaces every record associated with that number — across all events and stations. They choose which one to open and work on. The curator does not need to re-enroll them for each new event; enrollment is a one-time action per person.

🎤
Arturo — Conference Organizer
ProductCamp Austin · 15–25 speaker candidates per event

Every ProductCamp event draws 15 to 25 speaker candidates — product managers, academics, consultants. Right now, those talks live in someone's phone video at best. It might be two or three weeks before PCA produces and uploads the videos to YouTube — and by then, the moment has passed for most of the audience who were in the room.

ONDA changes that. The recording is done, transcribed, and in the audience's hands before they leave the building. Here is the complete workflow from Arturo's perspective as Curator, through the speaker candidates as Contributors, to the audience on the day of the event.

A Magazine Publisher
Multi-title publication · Expert contributors across multiple topics

LeverageUp Media publishes more than one magazine — health, business, lifestyle, and others. Each is a distinct publication with its own contributors and editorial standards. As a Curator, Robin manages all of them from a single ONDA Nile station, organized into named collections — one per magazine title.

She invites each contributor to record their article in their own voice and links them to the specific publication they're submitting to. A health practitioner authorized for LeverageUp Health does not automatically appear in LeverageUp Business. Robin controls that separation.

This same model applies to any curator managing multiple topic areas. David, for example, might separate contributors into Services, Products, or Use Cases rather than grouping everything under one undifferentiated station.

A contributor enrolled in Robin's station may also be a speaker at a ProductCamp event. Their phone number links both records. When they sign in, they see all of it — and choose what to work on.

"Replay isn't replacing articles — it's giving them another dimension." — Robin Roberson, LeverageUp Media
The LeverageUp workflow

Robin registers as a Curator Curator
One-time setup: LeverageUp Media curator station name, location, color theme, and LinkedIn profile as the human-curation gate.

Robin creates issues inside her station — for example, LeverageUp Health Issue 1 or LeverageUp Business Issue 1. Each issue carries a publication name (e.g. "LeverageUp Health") that groups it with other issues of the same magazine. This is the same step as Arturo creating PCA35 — the structure is identical, just named differently. Robin can have as many publications and issues as she has magazines.

Robin enrolls each contributor by phone number Curator
Robin adds each contributor's phone number to the system, authorizing them to submit to a specific issue. This is the same step Arturo takes with his speaker candidates — the curator confirms who is allowed in before anyone can sign in. If a contributor is already in the system from another station or issue, they are simply authorized for this new one. No duplicate record is created.

Contributors subscribe to ONDA Replay ($8.99/mo) Contributor

Contributors sign in and fill out their card Contributor
They open ONDA Replay, enter their phone number, receive a one-time code by text, and verify it. The app surfaces all their records across ONDA Nile. They select the issue they are authorized for and fill out their card: name, photo, bio, article title, and synopsis.
→ cards.html (Robin's review view)
6
Robin reviews each card for completeness Curator
She checks bio, article synopsis, and photo before anything goes public. Incomplete cards are flagged. Approved cards are visible on ONDA Nile.
7
Contributors record and share their .onda file Contributor
Each expert records their article in their own voice using ONDA Replay and shares the .onda file from Utilities directly to Robin's curator station queue. Recorded on their device. Not uploaded from YouTube.
8
Robin and the contributor reach agreement on the final recording Curator
Robin reviews the recording and may send feedback — a section that needs clarification, an intro that should be re-recorded. The contributor responds and resubmits if needed. This is an editorial conversation, not a unilateral decision. The content is the contributor's intellectual property. Nothing goes live until both sides are satisfied.
9
Approved files go live on the LeverageUp curator station Curator
The station page on ONDA Nile shows every article — title, author, synopsis — with a Listen in ONDA Replay button. If Robin wants to publish her own articles as audio, she can use bulk upload.
→ bulk-upload.html
10
Each contributor receives their .onda in their private archive Contributor
Their article, in their own voice, with a synchronized transcript — on their phone. A publishing credit they can share, and a copy they keep regardless of what Robin does with the station.

A Professional Network Hub
Field expertise · Colleagues with content worth sharing

Samar knows his field. He also knows colleagues who have built tutorials, explained processes, and answered the same client questions a hundred times. That knowledge exists. It just isn't organized anywhere.

Of the three curators in this document, Samar has the lightest ongoing workload. He sets up his curator station once, reaches out to colleagues, opens slots, and reviews what comes in. There is no recurring publication schedule, no event to run. The station simply grows as contributors add content.

A curator station with 50 files isn't a business card. It's a destination.

Anyone searching for how patents work finds Samar's curator station. They don't just find Samar — they find his entire network. Every contributor has a reason to send their own clients and contacts to the station.
