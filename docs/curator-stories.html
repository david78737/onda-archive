Three Ways a Curator Station Works

A Curator station on ONDA Nile is a curated library of audio content — searchable, playable, and shareable — built from the voices of people you know and trust. This document describes three real scenarios. Links to the actual forms are included so you can see what the workflow looks like in practice.

🎤
Arturo — Conference Organizer
ProductCamp Austin · 15–25 speaker candidates per event

Every ProductCamp event draws 15 to 25 speaker candidates — product managers, academics, consultants. Right now, those talks live in someone's phone video or nowhere at all. A week after the event, they're gone.

ONDA changes that. Here is the complete workflow from Arturo's perspective as Curator, through the speaker candidates as Presenters, to the audience on the day of the event.

The ProductCamp workflow — 11 steps
1
Arturo registers as a Curator Curator
He sets up the ProductCamp Austin curator station — name, location, color theme, his LinkedIn profile as the human-curation gate. One-time setup that carries forward to every future event.
→ register.html  ·  → station.html
2
Arturo creates the event and designated speaker slots Curator
He sets up PCA35 — date, venue, number of slots. Each slot is a placeholder waiting for a speaker candidate to claim it.
→ event.html
3
Arturo shares the event code Curator
He distributes the conference code to speaker candidates. Audience members will use the same code on the day of the event to receive .onda files by text message at the end of each presentation.
4
Speaker candidates subscribe to ONDA Replay ($8.99/mo) Presenter
The Contributor subscription gives them access to the presenter tools — the card form, the recording app, and the ability to submit to a Curator's event.
5
Speaker candidates fill out their application card Presenter
Each candidate submits their name, photo, bio, talk title, synopsis, and who the talk is for. They enter the event code to associate their card with PCA35.
→ cards.html (Arturo's review view)
6
Arturo reviews cards for completeness Curator
He checks each application — bio, synopsis, photo — before it goes public. Incomplete cards are flagged. Complete cards are approved and visible on ONDA Nile as "Coming Soon" before the event.
7
PCA protocol: the audience votes speakers in Audience
This is the ProductCamp unconference model — attendees vote on the day of the event for which candidates present. ONDA does not change this process; it works alongside it.
8
Voted-in presenters are assigned a room and time slot Presenter
Each slot corresponds to a card Arturo created during setup. The presenter enters the slot code into ONDA Replay — this arms their Stop button and links their recording to the correct card.
9
Audience members opt in by text Audience
At the start of each presentation, the presenter announces: "Text REPLAY to [number] to get this talk sent to your phone when I'm done." Audience members text in once. They're in.
10
Presenter taps Stop — everything uploads automatically Presenter
ONDA Replay uploads the audio file and the real-time transcript in one tap. No editing. No file management. The recording is tied to the presenter's card and the slot they were assigned.
11
Text messages go out to the audience Audience
Once transcription is complete, every opted-in audience member receives a text with a link to the .onda file. They open it in ONDA Replay: listen, read along, rewind, search the transcript. On their schedule, not the presenter's.
"The talk doesn't end when the presenter sits down. It lives in the curator station — discoverable by anyone who wasn't in the room."

Robin — Magazine Publisher
LeverageUp Media · Expert contributors across multiple topics

LeverageUp Media publishes articles written by experts — health practitioners, business coaches, specialists in their fields. There is a cohort who don't read well or tire easily. For them, an audio format is preferable — and often the difference between engaging with the content and skipping it entirely.

As a Curator, Robin invites each contributor to record their article in their own voice and upload the audio file. She reviews each recording, then publishes it to the LeverageUp curator station on ONDA Nile. Every article now has a button alongside it: Listen to this in ONDA Replay.

Some contributors may introduce unfamiliar technical terms, or speak with an accent some listeners find difficult to follow. The Whisper transcription engine is adept at converting challenging dialects into accurate English text — so the reader can listen, read along, or do both at once.

"Replay isn't replacing articles — it's giving them another dimension." — Robin Roberson, LeverageUp Media

Readers choose how they engage. The author's personality comes through in a way the printed page never could. Brenda explaining Bowen Therapy in her own voice is a different — and more compelling — experience than reading about it.

For Robin's contributors, being on ONDA Nile is a publishing credit. Their voice, their expertise, in a curated professional library — not buried in a YouTube algorithm.

The LeverageUp workflow — 10 steps
1
Robin registers as a Curator Curator
One-time setup: LeverageUp Media station name, location, color theme, LinkedIn profile as the human-curation gate.
→ register.html  ·  → station.html
2
Robin creates a slot for each contributor Curator
Each article gets a card — a placeholder with a title and a slot waiting to be filled. Robin decides how many contributors she's enrolling for this issue.
→ event.html
3
Robin shares a contributor code with each expert Curator
Each contributor receives a code that links their submission to their assigned card in the LeverageUp curator station.
4
Contributors fill out their card Contributor
Name, photo, bio, article title, synopsis, and who the article is for. They enter their contributor code to associate the card with the correct slot.
→ cards.html (Robin's review view)
5
Robin reviews each card for completeness Curator
She checks bio, article synopsis, and photo before anything goes public. Approved cards appear in the LeverageUp station — how Robin manages her publication schedule is her own editorial workflow, outside of ONDA.
6
Contributors record and share their .onda file via Utilities Contributor
Each expert records their article in their own voice using ONDA Replay and shares the .onda file from Utilities directly to Robin's curator station queue. Recorded on their device — not uploaded from YouTube.
7
Robin applies her editorial judgment Curator
In most cases the recording will be fine as-is. She can trim or modify audio in Audacity or CapCut — tools outside of ONDA Replay — and re-transcribe before it goes live. ONDA does not do audio editing. That craft stays with the curator.
8
Approved files are queued for the curator station Curator
Contributors shared their .onda directly from the app — Robin's approval moves them into the station. If Robin wants to publish her own articles as audio, she can use bulk upload — up to 5 at a time.
→ bulk-upload.html
9
Each article goes live on the LeverageUp curator station Curator
The station page on ONDA Nile shows every article — title, author, synopsis — with a Listen in ONDA Replay button. Robin maintains the station: updating profiles, adding new issues, managing visibility.
10
Each contributor receives their .onda in their private archive Contributor
Their article, in their own voice, with a synchronized transcript — on their phone. A publishing credit they can share, and a copy they keep regardless of what Robin does with the station.

Samar — Professional Network Hub
Patent law · Colleagues across the field with content worth sharing

Samar knows his field. He also knows colleagues who have built tutorials, explained processes, and answered the same client questions a hundred times. That knowledge exists. It just isn't organized anywhere.

Of the three curators in this document, Samar has the lightest ongoing workload. He sets up his curator station once, reaches out to colleagues, opens slots, and reviews what comes in. There is no recurring publication schedule, no event to run. The station simply grows as contributors add content — and once it reaches depth, it largely runs itself.

A curator station with 50 files isn't a business card. It's a destination.

Anyone searching for how patents work finds Samar's curator station. They don't just find Samar — they find his entire network. Every contributor has a reason to send their own clients and contacts to the station.

Their recording, their expertise, with a synchronized transcript — on their phone. A professional credential they can share with their own clients and contacts. Every contributor has a reason to point people to the curator station.
